## Finding 1 (measured, not theory): on 3 of 6 (instance × arm) cells, the officially-returned answer is NOT the "full" pass's own self-reported result — it is the earlier seed+repair candidate

Cross-checking each row's official `obj1/obj2/obj3/objective` against the raw
`info["passes"]` log:

| inst | arm | official obj1 | "full" pass's own self-reported z1 | seed+repair's own z1_after | returned solution = |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| prob_21 | baseline | 361 | 361 (exact match) | 362 | **full pass** (genuine, small improvement) |
| prob_21 | congestion | 311 | 311 (exact match) | 396 | **full pass** (genuine, large improvement) |
| prob_26 | baseline | 1,623 | 1,718 (worse) | **1,623 (exact match)** | **seed+repair** — full pass regressed and was discarded |
| prob_26 | congestion | 1,399 | 1,411 (worse) | **1,399 (exact match)** | **seed+repair** — full pass regressed and was discarded |
| prob_36 | baseline | 612 | 612 (exact match) | 678 | **full pass** (genuine improvement) |
| prob_36 | congestion | 487 | 498 (worse) | **487 (exact match)** | **seed+repair** — full pass regressed and was discarded |

This is not a bug in the sense of "internal objective disagrees with the
official checker" — `incumbent.py`'s `IncumbentStore.audit_and_update` is coded
exactly to this contract (docstring: "keeps the best *audited* solution;
anything unaudited can guide search but can never be returned") and every row's
`full_ran=True` above genuinely means *the stage executed*, not that its output
was returned. What this measures is: **on prob_26 (both arms) and prob_36
(congestion arm only), the additional master/LBBD-refinement or
rescue-repack-and-repair-again processing inside the "full" stage made the
tardiness component worse than the earlier, cheaper seed+repair construction,
and the incumbent store correctly fell back.** This is the standing finding F9
("O1/full pass is inert or *regressive* on easy instances" — BLINDSPOT_PASS
_2026-07-25.md), now observed **deterministically (N=3) at t=300 on a mid/tail
instance panel** (prob_26, prob_36) rather than only on the easy tier F9 was
originally scoped to (prob_3 was its cited example) — this D3 panel extends F9's
evidence base, it does not introduce a new mechanism.

## Finding 2 (measured, exact): wherever congestion wins at t=300 in this panel, 100% of the win is a Z1 (tardiness) effect, and the layer (w2·obj2 + w3·obj3) is worse under congestion in every case

Per-instance weights (`train/prob_*.json["weights"]`): prob_21 `w1=13333,
w2=10, w3=150`; prob_26 `w1=13333, w2=7, w3=150`; prob_36 `w1=667, w2=1, w3=13`.

| inst | Δ objective (congestion − baseline) | Δ Z1 = w1·Δobj1 | Δ layer = w2·Δobj2 + w3·Δobj3 | check (ΔZ1+Δlayer) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| prob_21 | −605,270 (congestion better) | **−666,650** (favors congestion) | +61,380 (favors baseline) | −605,270 ✓ exact |
| prob_26 | −2,881,650 (congestion better) | **−2,986,592** (favors congestion) | +104,942 (favors baseline) | −2,881,650 ✓ exact |
| prob_36 | −71,498 (congestion better) | **−83,375** (favors congestion) | +11,877 (favors baseline) | −71,498 ✓ exact |

Every single instance in this panel shows the identical qualitative shape:
congestion's win is entirely a tardiness effect, and its imbalance+preference
layer — the exact quantities the congestion-aware assignment was built to
improve — is *worse* than baseline's in every case where congestion wins. The
win survives only because `w1` dwarfs `w2, w3` by 50×–1900× on these instances.
This matches the direction (not magnitude) of the loss-case pattern D1 already
found on prob_38 ("congestion drives obj2 down sharply but obj3 up sharply...
net worse") and the ◆0.4 note on prob_21 ("areal signal anti-correlated with
the actual weighted objective") — except here, on the SAME three win/mixed
instances, it is Z1, not Z2, doing the work in every win case measured. This is
an observation, not a mechanism explanation — I have not traced *why*
congestion's construction produces lower tardiness at the cost of imbalance
here; that is a design question, not a measurement one.

## Answering the task's two questions

**Q1 — is the t=300 reversal general across the arm's t=60 win set?**

First, a correction the data forces: **prob_21 was not actually in the arm's
t=60 win set.** Pulling the N=5 t=60 table (`results/2026-07-27_f17_arm_ab.csv`):

| inst | t=60 winner | t=60 margin | t=300 winner (this panel) | t=300 margin | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| prob_21 | **baseline** (median 5,287,506 vs 5,719,218) | congestion loses by ~8.2% | **congestion** | congestion wins by ~11.6% | **direction flips, but in the OPPOSITE sense from the reversal**: baseline wins short, congestion wins long — not part of the documented reversal pattern at all |
| prob_26 | **congestion** (median 20,133,059 vs 23,398,462) | congestion wins by ~14.0% | **congestion** | congestion wins by ~13.0% | **ARM HOLDS** — margin essentially unchanged, no reversal |
| prob_36 | **congestion** (median 447,856 vs 865,899) | congestion wins by ~48.3% | **congestion** | congestion wins by ~14.1% | **ARM HOLDS DIRECTION, margin collapses >3×** — no sign reversal, but the win shrinks sharply |

So, of the three instances measured here, **two are genuinely in the arm's t=60
win set (prob_26, prob_36) and neither reverses** — the prob_31/prob_38
baseline-overtakes-at-t=300 pattern (established by prior work, not re-run
here) does **not** replicate on either. The third instance (prob_21) is not
actually a win-set member and shows a distinct, previously undocumented
inverse-flip pattern.

**Verdict on Q1: REVERSAL PARTIAL.** Confirmed (by prior, cited work) on
prob_31 and prob_38; **refuted as a general property of the arm's t=60 win set**
by prob_26 (flat) and prob_36 (holds, shrinks) in this panel. The reversal is
instance-specific, not a universal t=60-vs-t=300 property of the congestion
arm. prob_21's inverse-flip is a fourth, distinct pattern not covered by either
"reversal" or "arm holds" — worth its own bucket, not folded into either.

**prob_36 mechanism for the margin collapse** (measured, via Finding 1): at
t=60 baseline's full/master pass was budget-skipped for *both* arms
(`budget 5.5–6.0s < expected 16–17s`, confirmed in `2026-07-28_d2_screening
_sweep.csv`), so the t=60 comparison was baseline-greedy-seed+repair vs
congestion-greedy-seed+repair with nothing else running — a clean seed-level
comparison. At t=300 baseline's full pass is no longer skipped and its master
delivers a real, large gain (678→612 tardiness, seed+repair→full, table
above), closing most — but not all — of the gap; congestion's own t=300 answer
is *still* its seed+repair construction (its "full"-stage rescue-repack
regressed and was discarded, per Finding 1), so its t=60→t=300 improvement
is small (487→487 is flat on Z1; the ~2.3% objective gain from 447,856→437,468
comes from extra unhurried repair/polish time on the same seed, not from any
new mechanism unlocking).

**Q2 — what is the win feature (prob_21 and prob_26 share identical areal-fill
signatures but split at t=60)?**

I confirm the split as stated by the task (from
`BLINDSPOT_PASS_2026-07-27b_f17_arm_scoring.md` §5(a), not re-derived here):
prob_21 and prob_26 share `fill_grd = 1.79 → 1.00` and land on opposite sides at
t=60 — that document already concludes "the proposed predictor (areal fill
reduction) is falsified... do not promote on a feature we cannot name," and
this panel does not overturn that. What this panel adds, from the t=300 data
and Finding 1/2 above:

- **Both instances' wins (where congestion wins) are 100% Z1-driven, 0% (or
  negative) layer-driven** — that part of the "win feature" is now measured
  precisely on both, not just asserted directionally.
- **The two instances differ sharply in provenance, not in feature space**:
  on prob_21 the master/LBBD full-pass machinery genuinely earns its keep for
  *both* arms (Finding 1: `MATCH_FULL=True` both arms); on prob_26 it earns its
  keep for *neither* arm (`MATCH_FULL=False` both arms — the entire t=300
  comparison on prob_26 is actually a seed+repair-vs-seed+repair comparison,
  with the master/rescue-repack layer contributing nothing to either side's
  returned answer). This is a real, measured structural difference between the
  two instances that the areal-fill signature does not capture, and it directly
  explains why the arm's win *shrinks* on prob_36 (where the asymmetry is
  baseline-only) but stays *flat* on prob_26 (where the asymmetry is
  symmetric — neither arm's refinement layer survives audit, so extra budget
  buys neither arm anything new relative to the other). I am reporting this
  as an observation; I have not identified what instance property predicts
  which of the two provenance regimes (full-survives vs full-regresses) a
  given instance falls into — that remains an open, unnamed feature, same as
  the areal-fill question it replaces.

## Hybrid candidate (congestion-seed / master-full) — prob_36 Group-B advantage

**No "hybrid" arm exists in the code.** `solver/api.py`'s `_ARMS =
("baseline", "congestion")` — only these two are selectable; this panel could
not and did not directly measure the hybrid described in
`BLINDSPOT_PASS_2026-07-27b_f17_arm_scoring.md` §6 ("use the congestion
assignment for the SEED pass only, and leave the full pass on the master").
Everything below is **inference from this panel's baseline/congestion numbers
plus a read of `incumbent.py`'s selection logic — not a direct measurement**,
and should be read with that caveat throughout.

That prior document asserts: "where the full pass runs (t=300) it [the hybrid]
reproduces the baseline bit for bit." Reading `IncumbentStore.audit_and_update`
(`solver/incumbent.py`): it keeps whichever solution — from *any* pass, in
whatever order they ran — scores best under the **official** audit. A
hybrid's pipeline would accumulate a congestion-seeded seed+repair candidate
into the store *before* its master-driven full pass runs. If that later
master-driven candidate does not beat the congestion-seeded candidate under
official audit, the store keeps the earlier (congestion) one — the hybrid does
**not** automatically collapse to baseline just because the full pass "runs."

Applying that mechanic to this panel's numbers: on **prob_36**, baseline's own
t=300 full-pass answer (508,966) is *worse* than congestion's own t=300 answer
(437,468, itself a seed+repair candidate per Finding 1). If a hybrid's
master-driven full-pass candidate on prob_36 is close to baseline's own
(508,966) — a reasonable but unverified assumption, since it would be the same
master solving the same instance — it would **lose** the incumbent-store
comparison against the hybrid's own earlier congestion-seeded candidate, and
the hybrid's prob_36 answer would most likely **retain** something close to
congestion's construction, not baseline's. This is the **opposite** of what
task 0.4c's framing anticipated ("at t=300 it likely flips groups, which
decides whether the hybrid's best row survives") and the opposite of what I
first (incorrectly) concluded mid-analysis before re-reading
`incumbent.py` carefully. The same mechanic applies to prob_26 for the same
reason (baseline's own full pass there is *also* worse than congestion's
seed+repair answer).

**I am not rendering a verdict on the hybrid** — that requires an actual
hybrid arm, implemented and measured, not an inference from two other arms'
numbers plus a code read. What I am surfacing is a **specific, reviewable
correction-candidate** to a load-bearing claim in
`BLINDSPOT_PASS_2026-07-27b_f17_arm_scoring.md` §6 (the "reproduces baseline
bit for bit" line), grounded in this panel's own measured baseline/congestion
numbers on the exact instance (prob_36) that claim was extrapolated to. This
needs a rex pass on the hybrid claim itself, or a real hybrid-arm
implementation + measurement, before either version of the claim is treated
as settled.

## Verdict

**REVERSAL PARTIAL.** Confirmed on prob_31/prob_38 (prior, cited work, not
re-run); **does not replicate** on prob_26 (arm holds flat, ~14.0%→~13.0%) or
prob_36 (arm holds direction, margin collapses ~48.3%→~14.1%) in this panel.
prob_21 shows a fourth, distinct pattern — baseline wins at t=60, congestion
wins at t=300 — the inverse of the documented reversal, on an instance that was
never actually in the arm's t=60 win set. **The t=300 reversal is not a
general property of the arm's t=60 win set.**

**Win feature**: not areal fill (already falsified elsewhere, confirmed here
by omission — not re-tested). Measured instead: (1) every congestion win in
this panel is 100% Z1-driven, with the layer term working *against*
congestion in every case (exact decomposition above); (2) prob_21 and prob_26
differ in **provenance**, not feature space — on prob_21 the master/LBBD
full-pass machinery genuinely improves the answer for both arms; on prob_26 it
regresses for both arms and gets silently discarded by the incumbent-store
audit gate (Finding 1, connects to standing finding F9). No instance-level
predictor for which regime an instance falls into is identified here.

**Hybrid / Group-B**: **not measured** (no hybrid arm exists to run).
Inference from this panel's numbers plus the incumbent-store's actual
selection code suggests the prior "reproduces baseline bit for bit whenever
the full pass runs" claim may not hold on prob_36 specifically — the opposite
direction (hybrid retains something close to congestion's win) looks more
consistent with the measured baseline-loses-to-congestion-at-t=300 numbers on
this instance. This is flagged as a correction-candidate for review, not a
finding this panel can certify.

**What this does NOT show**: this is three instances (prob_21, prob_26,
prob_36) at one timelimit (t=300), N=3 (direction-grade, not ship-grade), on
an unisolated, load-compromised, and — for part of the run — not-exclusively-
occupied macOS box, with a ~3-hour session gap splitting the panel into two
sub-runs. It does not clear or reject the F17 arm for promotion (gated on
D1+D2+D3+M1 per ◆0.4; this is D3 of that set). It does not measure the hybrid
candidate directly. It does not diagnose *why* the master/LBBD full-pass
machinery regresses on prob_26/prob_36-congestion while it improves on
prob_21/prob_36-baseline — that is a design question for tom/rex, not
something this panel resolves.
