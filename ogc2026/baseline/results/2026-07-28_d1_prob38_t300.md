## HYPOTHESIS scoring (from BLINDSPOT_PASS_2026-07-27b_f17_arm_scoring.md §4)

> HYPOTHESIS: if the +36% wall inflation observed on prob_31 @ t=300
> generalizes, prob_38 @ t=300 under the congestion arm lands near ~370s,
> past the 305s hard external kill → -1.

**REFUTED on prob_38, and not marginally.** Observed congestion wall
(271.8–271.9s, zero variance across 3 reps) is ~98s *below* the hypothesized
~370s and sits 33s inside the 305s hard-kill convention — a gap far larger
than the recorded 14–27% contention noise band (14–27% of 272s is 38–73s; the
observed-vs-hypothesized gap is ~36%, i.e. almost exactly the inverse of the
prob_31 inflation number that motivated the hypothesis, meaning: on prob_38
the inflation this hypothesis predicted **did not occur at all**). The +36%
wall-inflation effect measured on prob_31 does not generalize to prob_38.
Congestion's wall on prob_38 is essentially indistinguishable from baseline's
(within 0.5s), and if anything came in fractionally *lower* and with *zero*
rep-to-rep variance (baseline varied ~0.5s and flip-flopped whether the
CP-SAT master finished OPTIMAL). This refutation held even as machine load
rose during the run (see instrument caveat above) — a load-only-inflates
world would have made the hypothesis *more* likely to fire, not less, so this
is conservative-valid direction evidence, not something that needs an idle
re-run to overturn on the kill question specifically.

## Secondary finding, reported loudly per the task brief: baseline ALSO breaches the 0.90·t margin — again

Every one of the 6 main-panel reps — **both arms** — landed at 271.8–272.3s,
which is *past* the 270s (0.90·300) convention this project uses for
submit-safety margin. This replicates the single crashed-panel data point
(baseline 272.1s) cited in the task brief, now with N=3 confirmation on
baseline and N=3 on congestion showing the identical pattern. Two things
follow:

1. **This is not an arm effect.** Congestion's median margin breach (-1.85s)
   is not worse than baseline's (-2.07s) — if anything very slightly better.
   Whatever is driving the margin breach, both arms share it equally. This
   is task **0.4e's finding** (a live submit-safety issue in the current
   solver, independent of the F17 arm), not new evidence against or for
   promoting the congestion arm.
2. **There is a structural (non-load) candidate explanation, worth surfacing
   even though I only measure, I don't diagnose**: `solver/budget.py` sets
   `SAFETY_FACTOR = 0.93`, giving an internal effective budget of
   `max(1.0, 300*0.93 - 1.0) = 278.0s` — a number that is *already above* the
   270s (`0.90·t`) convention used for the stamping margin column. A run that
   legitimately uses close to its full internal budget plus a small
   audit/emit tail will land around ~272–280s by construction, independent of
   machine load. The six main-panel walls (271.8–272.3s, tight band, rising
   load notwithstanding) are consistent with that read: this looks like the
   solver hitting its own designed ceiling, not a load-driven excursion. I
   flag this as an observation from the numbers and the one budget constant
   CLAUDE.md documents (`max(1.0, timelimit*0.93-1.0)`) — the actual root
   cause of the -2s margin (structural budget-ceiling vs load-inflated
   audit/pack tail vs both) is a diagnosis question for 0.4e, not something
   this panel resolves.

Per the load-aware decision asymmetry rule in my instructions: because this
IS a breach (not a comfortable pass) observed under a compromised machine, it
is reported prominently here but marked **NEEDS IDLE-MACHINE CONFIRMATION**
for the margin-breach magnitude specifically (is it -2s or more/less on a
quiet Linux 4-core box) — while the kill-hypothesis refutation above does not
carry that caveat, because the margin to an actual kill (33s) is far larger
than any load-driven swing on record.

At t=300 on prob_38, baseline's median objective (65,906,720) beats
congestion's (70,198,763) by ~6.5%. This replicates the direction of the
◆0.4-decided t=300 reversal already on record for prob_31 (baseline wins at

persistent effect). Component breakdown is consistent with the standing
story: congestion drives obj2 (bay-load imbalance) down sharply (1,135 vs
3,712–4,262) but obj3 (bay-preference penalty) up sharply (6,394 vs
1,624–1,983) and obj1 (tardiness) up slightly (5,121 vs 4,765–5,043) — net
worse on the weighted objective. This is a second data point (after prob_31)
for the "areal/congestion signal anti-correlated with the actual weighted
objective at long budgets" pattern already flagged for prob_21 in
◆0.4/1.6.

## Verdict

**HYPOTHESIS REFUTED** on prob_38 @ t=300: the congestion arm does not
approach the ~370s figure and does not come anywhere near the 305s hard-kill
convention (33s of margin observed, N=3, zero variance). No -1 occurred on
either arm, on any of the 8 reps run (6 main + 2 calibration). This finding
is **local-only, not gauntlet-valid** (macOS, 8-core, unisolated, swap+load
active) but is conservative-valid direction evidence given load rose rather
than fell during the run and walls stayed flat.

**Separately and reported loudly**: both arms breach the 0.90·t=270s
submit-safety margin by ~2s at t=300 on prob_38, replicating the prior
crashed-panel baseline data point — this is task 0.4e's finding, independent
of the F17 arm, and is flagged NEEDS-IDLE-MACHINE-CONFIRMATION for exact
magnitude, with a structural (budget-constant) candidate explanation noted
above for whoever picks up 0.4e.

**What this does NOT show**: this is one instance (prob_38) at one timelimit
(300s), N=3 (direction-grade, not ship-grade — N≥5 required for that), on an
unisolated, load-compromised macOS box. It does not clear the F17 arm for
promotion (that is gated on D1+D2+D3+M1 per ◆0.4, and this is D1 only), does
not resolve why the "full" pass didn't complete on 4/6 main-panel reps (an
instrumentation gap I flagged, not chased further here), and does not
diagnose the root cause of the 270s-margin breach (budget-ceiling structure
vs load vs both) — that diagnosis belongs to 0.4e, not to this panel.
